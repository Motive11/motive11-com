Privacy Policy

Most privacy policies are written to cover the company. This one is written to tell you what actually happens. The short version is that this website collects nothing about you personally, and the information that does matter is the material you hand over once you become a client.

Last updated August 16, 2026

1The short version

  • This website sets no cookies and collects no personal data. There is no form on it, no signup, no tracking pixel, and no advertising network.
  • Analytics are anonymous and aggregate. I can see that a page was viewed. I cannot see who viewed it.
  • The information worth caring about is what you send me directly: emails, files, documents, and access to your systems during an engagement.
  • That material goes through AI providers as part of how the work gets done. The AI Policy explains exactly how, and it is worth reading before the rest of this page.
  • Nothing is sold, rented, or handed to advertisers. Ever. There is no version of this business where that makes sense.

2What this website collects

Almost nothing, and that is deliberate. motive11.com is a single page that points people to markszymanski.co. It has no contact form, no newsletter box, and no account system, so there is nothing on it that asks you for information.

Traffic is measured with Fathom Analytics, which is cookieless by design. It does not set cookies, does not fingerprint you, does not retain IP addresses, and does not follow you to other sites. What it produces is counts: pages viewed, rough referrer, rough country, device type. None of it identifies a person.

Because there are no cookies and no personal data, this site has no cookie banner. That is not an oversight. There is nothing to ask your consent for.

Three technical things do see your IP address, because serving you a web page is impossible without it: Cloudflare, which hosts the site, and the content delivery networks that serve the typeface and the animation library. That is standard infrastructure logging, it is not used to build a profile of you, and I do not have access to it as an identifiable record.

3When you contact me

Contact happens by email, or through the form at markszymanski.co, which is a separate site with its own policies. If you email me, I have your email, your name, and whatever you chose to put in the message. That sits in my mailbox the way any business correspondence does.

I use what you send to answer you and, if it goes somewhere, to scope and quote the work. I do not add you to a marketing list because you emailed me. Motive11 does not run one.

4When you become a client

This is the part that actually matters, and it is not really about the website at all. During an engagement you hand over real material: documents, spreadsheets, customer data, email threads, analytics, credentials, and access to systems you own.

That material is used to do your project and for nothing else. It is not published, not shared with other clients, and not used to build something I sell to someone else. Where work becomes a case study, it goes through you first and gets written the way you approve it.

Your material is processed by AI providers. That is central enough to how the work is produced that it has its own page. The AI Policy covers what is transmitted, which provider controls are enabled, what is never sent, how to exclude specific material, and what you own at the end. It is part of the Terms of Service and is incorporated by reference.

If your project involves personal information about your customers, which is common, it is handled as your data and not mine. I do not use it for any purpose outside your project, and if you need a separate data processing agreement in place, ask and we will sign one.

5What never gets collected

  • Card numbers. Payments run through Stripe. Card details go to Stripe directly and I never see or store them. What I get back is the fact that an invoice was paid, and the billing name and email attached to it.
  • Government identifiers. Social security numbers, tax identifiers, licenses, passports. There is no reason for this business to hold any of it, so it does not.
  • Credentials sent to AI providers. Passwords, keys, and access tokens are used to operate your systems and are not transmitted to AI providers. This is stated in the AI Policy too, because it is worth saying twice.
  • Anything from a tracking network. No advertising pixels, no retargeting, no data brokers, no third-party audience tools.

6Who else touches your information

A one-person business still runs on software built by other people. These are the services that can come into contact with your information, and what each one is for:

  • Google Workspace hosts my email, so any correspondence with me lives there.
  • Stripe processes payments and holds the billing record.
  • Postmark sends transactional email, meaning things like confirmations and notifications. It is not a marketing platform and is never used as one.
  • Cloudflare hosts this site and much of what gets built.
  • Fathom Analytics counts page views, anonymously, as described above.
  • AI providers, currently Anthropic as the main one, process material during an engagement. See the AI Policy.

Each of these has its own terms and its own privacy policy, and those govern what they do with data they hold. I can tell you what Motive11 does and which controls are enabled. I cannot make promises on a third party's behalf, and you should be suspicious of anyone who does.

7How long things are kept

Working files and correspondence for a project are kept for the life of the engagement and for a reasonable period after it, because clients come back and asking you to re-send everything is a bad experience. Invoices, contracts, and financial records are kept as long as tax and business law requires, which is several years.

If you want your material deleted sooner, say so. Anything I am not legally required to keep gets removed, and I will tell you plainly what has to stay and why.

8Your choices

You can ask me what information I hold about you, ask for it to be corrected, ask for a copy, or ask for it to be deleted. There is no portal and no form for this. Email hello@motive11.com and you will get a real answer from the person who actually has the files.

If you are somewhere with specific statutory data rights, including the EU and UK under GDPR or California under the CCPA, those rights apply to you and the way to exercise them is the same email. Motive11 does not sell personal information, so there is nothing for you to opt out of on that front.

9Security

Accounts are protected with strong unique credentials and multi-factor authentication, devices are encrypted, and client credentials are stored in a password manager rather than in a document or an email thread.

No one can honestly promise perfect security, and a policy that claims it is telling you something false on the page where it should be most careful. What I can promise is that if something involving your material goes wrong, you hear it from me quickly and directly.

10Children

This is a business to business service. It is not directed at children, and information about children is not knowingly collected. If you believe a child has sent me information, email me and it will be deleted.

11Changes

This page gets updated when what I actually do changes, and the date at the top moves with it. If a change is significant and you are a current client, you get told directly rather than left to notice a new date on a web page.

12Contact

Motive11 LLC is a Pennsylvania limited liability company based in Pittsburgh, and this policy is governed by Pennsylvania law. Questions about any of it are welcome, including the awkward ones, and the answer will be a straight one.
